🇷🇺 Русский Час 8:30 AM
«Все счастливые семьи похожи друг на друга, каждая несчастливая семья несчастлива по-своему.»
Vse schastlívye sem'í pokhózhi drug na drúga, kázhdaya neschastlívaya sem'yá neschastlíva po-svóemu.
похожи друг на друга — "resemble one another," literally "similar friend onto friend." The construction друг на друга is a reciprocal pronoun built from repeating друг (friend) with different case prepositions. Russian builds mutuality from friendship. Compare English "each other" — which builds it from arithmetic.

Königsberg → Kaliningrad
54.7104° N, 20.4522° E
Renamed in 1946 after Mikhail Kalinin, titular head of the Soviet state. The German population was expelled; the city's 700-year Prussian identity was erased by decree. Kant was born here, spent his entire life within its limits, and is still buried here — a German philosopher in a Russian city named after a Soviet bureaucrat. The river Pregel became the Pregolya. ✦ The Untranslatable 3:00 PM
木漏れ日
Japanese · komorebi
The sunlight that filters through the leaves of trees. Not dappled light (that's about the pattern on the ground). Not a sunbeam (that's about the ray itself). Komorebi is about the event — light and leaves and air collaborating in a specific way. 木 (tree) + 漏れ (leak through) + 日 (sun/day). The light is conceived as something the tree permits rather than something the sun does.

⟺ The Diff Monday
Rilke, Duino Elegies I, opening
Stephen Mitchell, 1982 Who, if I cried out, would hear me among the Angels' hierarchies?
Edward Snow, 2000 Who, if I screamed, would hear me among the angelic orders?
"Cried out" vs "screamed" — Mitchell softens schriee, which is raw, animal, throat-tearing. Snow preserves the violence. "Hierarchies" vs "orders" — both translate Ordnungen, but "hierarchies" implies ranked power; "orders" suggests something more monastic, organized but not necessarily vertical. Mitchell's angel is a CEO; Snow's is a monk.
✝ The Dead Medium Friday
Pneumatic Tube Mail
1853–1953 · Paris, New York, Berlin, Prague, London
Messages in capsules, fired through underground tubes at 35mph by compressed air. At its peak, the Paris network moved 150,000 messages per day — faster than email, if you only needed to reach someone across the city. What died with it wasn't speed but physicality: someone on the other end held a capsule that had been in your hands minutes before. The medium carried touch.
◈ Typeface of the Week Wednesday
Aa Бб Жж Щщ
PT Serif
Alexandra Korolkova & Olga Umpeleva · ParaType · 2010
Commissioned by the Russian government as part of a national program to create free fonts with full Cyrillic support. The brief was political: ensure every Russian citizen could produce typographically decent documents without paying for fonts. PT Serif handles the notoriously difficult Cyrillic letterforms (Ж, Щ, Д) with unusual grace. One of the few typefaces where the Cyrillic feels native rather than adapted.
🍳 Recipe of the Week Wednesday
Shchi (Щи) — Cabbage Soup
From Proto-Slavic *sъtьje, meaning "nourishment" or "juice." Cognate with Lithuanian sùtis (juice) and possibly Latin sūcus. The word predates the recipe — people named the concept of being nourished before they settled on cabbage as the vehicle.
The kind of thing you make on a grey February Wednesday when the forecast is 34°F and honesty. Sauerkraut base, not fresh cabbage — the fermentation gives it depth. Bone broth, potato, carrot, dill. An hour of simmering. Serve with sour cream and dark bread. Feeds four, or one person for three days.
